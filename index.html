Behind the numbers

How the planner works

  1. 1

    Build a plan

    The computer finds the cheapest number of staff that still follows the hospital rules. This maths method is called linear programming.

  2. 2

    Test the plan

    We play out 2,000 made-up days: some busy, some quiet, some with staff off sick or a big emergency. This is called a simulation.

  3. 3

    Add up the real cost

    Wages and beds, plus overtime, plus ₹6,000 for a patient not seen in time, ₹9,500 for a patient sent away and ₹1,200 for each hour waiting for a bed.

Hospital rules the plans follow
  • At least 1 doctor for every 2 nurses.
  • At least 2 technicians per shift, and at least 4 doctors at night.
  • Planned staff must be able to see at least 95% of the patients we expect.
  • A doctor sees about 18 patients a shift, a nurse 12 and a technician 30. Very sick patients count as 1.5.
